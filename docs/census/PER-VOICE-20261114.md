# What a voice costs, fixed and per voice, 2026-11-14

Scenario `wide84` and its 6 and 24-key cuts (keys struck 200 frames apart, held to frame 240000; 64-frame
blocks; corpus build, nice 19, machine load 30 to 50). Cost per block is the audio thread's CPU over the blocks
where every key is held; a line through the 6-key and 84-key runs splits it into a fixed part (reverb bus,
scripts) and a marginal part per voice. Raw wall times swing with the load, so the cost of a change is also
counted in `cycles:u` samples (one per 2 M cycles) over the whole run, where load does not move it.

| Library | Fixed per block | Marginal per voice-block, before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| ANALOG STRINGS | 72 µs | 3.87 µs | 3.88 µs |
| Areia Full Ens | 22 µs | 0.95 µs | 0.83 µs |
| AO Full Ensemble | 86 µs | 6.65 µs | 4.31 µs |
| Clarinet A | 167 µs | 0.91 µs | 0.74 µs |

The earlier "3 to 5 times Areia" for the clarinet was its fixed cost: a convolution reverb of 163 µs a block is
12 % of a core with ten voices playing. Its marginal voice is cheaper than Areia's. The real per-voice costs are
ANALOG (3.9 µs: per-voice filter, chorus and insert chain) and AO (4.3 µs, 6.65 before).

## What AO's voice computed

A 5 s run made 164 million LFO evaluations, all free-running sines. Each voice refresh reads every smoothed route
(a step wave's one-pole, done as 20 taps so it needs no state and follows the tempo) at two ages; a voice's rack
reads its own routes the same way. A free-running wave at a given time depends only on the time, phase and rate,
so every voice computed the same 20 numbers.

## Candidates, measured on AO

| Candidate | Result |
|---|---|
| Cheaper wave: no libm `fmod` for the fraction, no `floor` unless the wave is random | Exact; the wave fell from 20 % of AO's audio thread to 4 %, a small gain on its own |
| Cache each wave value by (time, phase, rate, wave) | Slower: the lookup (hash, thread-local) cost 13 % against 3.6 % for the sine it saved. The cost is the number of calls, not one call |
| Share the whole smoothed result by its inputs (transfer table, smoothing, wave, time, scale) | **Kept.** One lookup replaces 20 evaluations: 95 % hit rate on AO; audio-thread cycles −24 % (56.3 G to 43.0 G over the run, load included); marginal 6.65 to 4.31 µs |
| A stateful one-pole per voice, or the closed form of a smoothed sine | Not taken: O(1) but not bit-identical (the 20-tap window truncates at 5 time constants), and a state per voice loses "follows the tempo with no state" |

Audio is bit-identical on all four libraries (FNV bc6601d8f67cb2a4, 5d7644528e48aacc, 3140b96c0adb6e1a,
55c7c0669aa5cc09).

## FLAC page reads, looked at again

The earlier proposal (read whole packets so each decodes once) rested on a wrong premise: the stream store already
reads up to 16 consecutive requested pages in one read (`RUN` in store.rs), so a packet decodes about once. What
costs is each read opening the file and seeking: symphonia binary-searches the file, resyncing from the middle of
the stream at each step, because UVI's FLAC has no seek table. `resync` and `parse` are about half of the stream
thread's cycles on AO. A per-sample cursor kept bit-identical audio and saved 4 to 12 % of the stream thread for
26 to 40 MB of RAM: not taken. The candidate that fits is a sparse seek index per played sample (frame, byte
offset), built once from our own frame scan: about 1 KB a played sample and no RAM growth worth naming, as libFLAC
and ffmpeg do. Not built here.

## Next, for ANALOG

Its 3.9 µs is the voice itself: `read_window`, `render_through`, the per-voice SVF and chorus. The designs to compare
are four voices a lane through the SVF and the interpolator (sfizz and Vital batch voices through SIMD) against the
current one voice at a time; a Hermite against a windowed-sinc read on the same sample, for cost and aliasing. That
needs the voice's render loop split into a read phase and a filter phase, which is a PR of its own.
