# 300 voices at about 1 % CPU: measured gap, 2026-11-14

VISION.md's goal (not yet measured until now): about 300 voices at about 1 % CPU. Scenario
`wide84`: 84 keys struck 200 frames apart (keys 24..107, velocity 90), all held to frame 240000. Run:
`KONTRA_INSPECT_LEAN=1 KONTRA_INSPECT_BLOCK=64 kontra-inspect play LIB --scenario wide84.json`, corpus
build, nice 19, a busy 4-core machine (load 30+). CPU is the audio thread's CPU time per 64-frame block
(1333 µs at 48 kHz) over the blocks where all 84 keys are held. 1 % of a core is 13 µs per block.

| Library | Peak voices | Mean CPU per block | % of a core | p99 | Per voice per block |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ANALOG STRINGS (Kontakt) | 134 | 584 µs | 43.8 % | 1443 µs | 4.4 µs |
| Areia Full Ens (Kontakt) | 513 | 491 µs | 36.9 % | 661 µs | 1.0 µs |
| Augmented Orchestra Full Ens (UVI) | 239 | 753 µs | 56.5 % | 1439 µs | 3.2 µs |
| VWinds Clarinet A (UVI) | 42 | 200 µs | 15.0 % | 606 µs | 4.8 µs |

The gap to 300 voices at 1 % (13 µs a block, 0.04 µs a voice-block) is 20 to 100 times, by library. Areia
(plain sample playback, 1.0 µs a voice-block, 15 ns a voice-sample) is the floor today. A sample-interpolating voice
needs at least 2 to 5 ns a sample even in SIMD, which is 40 to 100 µs for 300 voices, so about 3 to 7 % of a core is the
physical floor for this scenario; 1 % holds only for voices that are idle, sustained on a shared
render, or not interpolating. Per voice per block, ANALOG, AO and Clarinet cost 3 to 5 times Areia: the
per-voice filter, modulation, and insert work, not sample reading.

Where it goes (perf, `cycles:u`, same scenario):
- ANALOG: `Voice::read_window` 9 %, `Voice::render_through` 8 %, `dsp_filter::Svf::process` 7 %,
  `Engine::process` 7 %, script VM 5 %, convolver 5 %.
- AO: the FLAC stream thread is 32 to 54 % of the whole process's cycles (`decode_rice_partition`,
  `PacketParser::resync`, parse). Each 1024-frame page read re-opens the file, re-reads the metadata,
  seeks and decodes a whole 4096-frame packet, so each packet decodes about four times. That is off
  the audio thread but is real CPU and the machine's load.

Tried: a per-sample FLAC read cursor (keep the open stream and last packet, continue without a seek).
Audio is bit-identical (FNV 3140b96c0adb6e1a before and after). Stream-thread user CPU on AO fell 4 % with 64
cursors and 12 % with 256; peak RSS rose 26 and 40 MB (each cursor holds the stream buffer, the decoder
and a decoded packet). Not taken: for RAM already at the budget the trade is not better. A cheaper form is
to read FLAC in whole-packet pages, so a packet decodes once.

Not measured: Kontakt's CPU and RAM on the same patches. That needs the CLAP host rig
(`tools/cpu-audit-native.py`) in a quiet window, which the machine has not had.
